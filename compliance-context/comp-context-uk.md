## Regulatory lens — what makes UK distinct

Read UK through an FCA and Consumer Duty lens. Consumer Duty is
the defining UK-specific pressure: the obligation to deliver good
outcomes, avoid foreseeable harm, and evidence both. Where an
analysis touches UK, always consider the Consumer Duty angle
even when no single hard rule is breached, because the Duty
operates at the outcome level, not just the rule level.

The UK has the strictest product prohibitions in the group on
crypto, which is the other defining feature.

## Entity-specific regulatory constraints

Verify the precise current rule by browsing before relying on
any of these.

- **Consumer Duty:** applies across the board. The four outcomes
  are products and services, price and value, consumer
  understanding, and consumer support. Treat foreseeable harm and
  good outcomes as live tests on any UK change, not just rule
  compliance.
- **Crypto ETN restricted investor regime:** retail access to
  crypto ETNs requires the client to self-certify as a restricted
  investor, with the associated risk warnings and the financial
  promotion rules for restricted mass market investments. Verify
  the current COBS position.
- **Appropriateness for complex products:** required for complex
  instruments under UK MiFID. CFDs and complex ETPs fall here.
- **Financial promotions regime:** the UK has a strict regime for
  high-risk investments, including the restricted mass market
  investment rules. Relevant whenever marketing or client-facing
  promotion of a higher-risk product is in scope.
